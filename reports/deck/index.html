Real data, real quirks

14,370radio clips after de-duplication (2018–2025)
148races of telemetry joined (FastF1)
82%of “box box” calls stamped after the car entered the pits
  • No 2022 season in the dataset; 2025 is thin (669 clips)
  • 204 byte-identical “Thank you.” clips reused across races: silent audio the original ASR hallucinated over
  • Broadcast timestamps lag speech by ~30–60 s (about half a lap), so all windows are lap-level

Where it goes wrong, and why

  • Incidents told as another driver's behaviour look like pace complaints
  • Brake or balance complaints (unverifiable) look like tyre complaints
  • “have a bit of contacts, just keep an eye on things.” → predicted GRIP_DEG, truly INCIDENT
  • “Alonso is moving over the place. understood, we'll report.” → predicted NO_CLAIM, truly INCIDENT
9 / 16
The confusions are semantic and taxonomic, not random. Several errors trace back to ASR mistakes.

Would more labels help the transformer?

  • Same race-grouped folds; training races subsampled to 25–100%
  • At full data: DeBERTa 0.43 vs TF-IDF 0.47
  • DeBERTa climbs twice as steeply, then flattens: no evidence it would overtake with modestly more labels
  • Both stay well below the zero-shot LLM (0.63)

Does the car back up the claim? Not beyond chance

  • Rules were fixed before any claim was labelled
  • No claim type beats its base rate (all CIs include 0)
  • Incident claims match race-control messages 2× the base rate, but just as often 3 laps before the message

Does arousal change the next laps?

  • Mixed model: residual over the next 3 laps on lagged arousal probabilities, controlling for the current lap
  • Effect: +0.147 s (CI -0.02 to +0.31); minimum detectable ±0.26 s, computed before fitting
  • Hand-label check: -0.005 s, so it doesn't even keep the direction
  • No evidence drivers differ (heterogeneity p = 0.19)

Null: effects under ~0.26 s/lap can't be detected with these data

What holds up, and what doesn't

Holds up

  • Claim type is identifiable from speech, on every split
  • A zero-shot LLM told the definitions beats fine-tuning (+0.20)
  • WavLM partial fine-tuning lifts audio arousal (+0.11)
  • Lap-time model works; timestamps lag speech ~30–60 s

Doesn't (yet)

  • Speaker attribution: 67% vs 85% gate
  • ASR prompting: ~3–4% WER gain vs a 15% bar
  • Telemetry doesn't corroborate claims (placebo equal)
  • Arousal doesn't detectably change pace (null)

Next: a second annotator, more rare-claim labels, and supervised attribution trained on our hand labels
